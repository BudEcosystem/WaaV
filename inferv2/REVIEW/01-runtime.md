## Headline (read first — it re-frames every severity below)

**The runtime crate is two crates wearing one coat.** A small *live* spine — `serve.rs` (`serve_codec_ar*`), `driver.rs` (`Driver`/`ActiveSet`/`IdleSignal`), `arstep.rs` (the `ArStepModel` seam minus `AcousticDelayRing`), and the `watchdog.rs` subset actually constructed by the server (`FrameWatchdog`, `LeakWatchdog`, `FrameLoop`, `CancelToken`, `SidecarHeartbeat`, `VramAccountant`, `CoLoadCalibrator`, `DcgmExporterDramActive`) — *is* on the GB10 serving path and is genuinely high quality: typed-error-not-panic is enforced almost everywhere, masked≠absent is honored at the driver seam, the RAII spine (`SpineSlots`) frees leases on every `?` path, and bit-identity between the multiplexed loop and the per-slot reference is live-gated.

Everything else is **`pub`-exported, unit-tested SHELFWARE with zero non-test constructors anywhere in the workspace**. Confirmed dead at the live path (grep over `crates/` for non-test callers returns nothing): `paged_kv::PagedKvTable`, `prefix_cache::{RadixPrefixCache, StreamingEncoderCache}`, `arstep::AcousticDelayRing`, all of `reasoning.rs` (`LatencyFiller`/`SentenceAggregator`/`LlmStreamNode`/`ToolCallNode`), all of `accel.rs`/`cuda_graph.rs`/`kernel_discipline.rs`/`graph_fallback.rs`/`cell.rs`, `numerics::sample_token`, `cfg_batch.rs`, `dynamic_fr.rs`, and the `watchdog.rs` defense legs `InputFirewall`/`DeadLetterSink`/`SourceRateLimiter`/`RecycleGate`/`CoMetric`/`FrameBudget`. (`turn.rs` is the one exception in the "small types" set: `TurnHead`/`DoubleTalkPolicy`/`TurnState` *are* consumed by the live duplex path.)

This matches the scheduler crate's verdict (`02-scheduler.md` CRITICAL-1): the module docs assert runtime guarantees the wiring does not deliver. The two consequences:
- **HIGH-1**: the GB10 resilience these modules promise (CUDA-graph fallback H4, crash-isolation J1, poison-pill firewall J17, long-form KV escape L12, on-token sampler) is **inert**. A capture failure / poison input / multi-minute turn on the real box is handled by whatever the *backend* does on its own, not by the reviewed code.
- The dead modules carry **real latent defects** (overflow, unbounded growth, narrowing) that will bite the instant they are wired — so they are filed here at their *when-live* severity with the `[suspected]`/shelfware caveat noted.

The live-path defects that bite **today** are CRITICAL-1 (shared-loop head-of-line block under a slow consumer — confirmed cross-crate), CRITICAL-2 (a release-build cross-tenant audio bleed in the live S2S step), and the HIGH cluster on the multiplexed loop (hung-consumer GPU waste, O(active²) scatter, datagram seq overflow).

## CRITICAL

### [CRITICAL] One slow consumer blocks the ENTIRE shared lockstep loop for up to 10 s — head-of-line stall across all 24 tenants `[confirmed]`
`serve.rs:776` (`drain_finished_stream` runs on the single mux thread) · `serve.rs:842-857` (sink called synchronously inside the decode `emit`) · consumer `codec_ar_batcher.rs:358-375` (`bounded_send` → `std::thread::sleep(2ms)` looped to `EGRESS_BACKPRESSURE_BUDGET = 10s`). Cross-ref `04-server-integration.md` HIGH.
- **What's wrong.** `serve_codec_ar_multiplexed_bounded` is the *live* shared loop (wired at `codec_ar_batcher.rs:331`). Its drain step (`serve.rs:771-778`) calls `drain_finished_stream`, which decodes a finished slot's body and pushes each PCM chunk through `(stream.admit.sink)(&ev)` **synchronously on the mux loop thread**. In production that sink is `bounded_send`, which on a full 64-deep egress channel parks the thread `sleep(2ms)` in a retry loop for up to **10 seconds**. While that one slow/half-open TCP consumer is being drained, the mux thread cannot drain other finished slots, cannot run `driver.tick` (the batched `step_batch`), cannot admit, and cannot park. The per-stream *memory* bound is correct; the *time* coupling is the bug.
- **Why it bites at enterprise scale.** This is the precise failure the multiplexed loop was built to *prevent* ("kills the per-request mutex", `serve.rs:433`). With `MAX_SLOTS = 24` and thousands of ragged streams, a handful of slow readers (mobile clients, congested links, half-open sockets that never send FIN) serialize the whole engine into 10 s × N-chunks stalls — every other tenant's audio freezes behind one stuck reader. Worse, the J16 frame watchdog can then trip the *innocent* stalled neighbours (no `record_progress` fires while the thread is asleep in `bounded_send`), cascading a single slow consumer into a fleet of false stall-kills.
- **Fix (bit-faithful).** The drain must never block the lockstep thread. Either (a) move per-stream egress off the loop: a small per-stream forwarder owns the bounded send + backpressure; the loop only `try_send`s and on `Full` hands the segment to that task; or (b) make the loop-thread send non-blocking `try_send` and on `Full` set `hung_up`/`SlowConsumer` immediately (shed, don't block) — accept that a wedged consumer loses audio rather than stall all 24 tenants. The 10 s blocking retry is only safe in the single-stream `serve_codec_ar_stream` path (which uses a tokio *unbounded* channel and never blocks — see MED-egress-unbounded), never in the mux loop. Numerics of every admitted stream are untouched (egress timing is not bit-identity).

### [CRITICAL] Live S2S duplex `step()` reassembles model-out by POSITION, not slot id — a release build silently cross-wires one tenant's audio into another's `[confirmed]`
`duplex_codec_ar.rs:297` (`active_iter.next().expect("an active slot has an active entry")`) and `:302` (`stepped.remove(&slot_id).expect("a continuing slot has a batched model-out")`); `debug_assert_eq!` at `:298` is compiled out in release. (Cross-crate: `waav-infer-core/src/s2s/duplex_codec_ar.rs`, the live `DuplexStepModel` driven by the engine on CUDA.)
- **What's wrong.** The reassembly loop walks `batch.slots` in order and `active_iter.next()`s one entry per *unmasked* slot, **asserting** that `active` (a `Vec<(usize, SlotId, …)>`) is in slot order. It is — but only because the build loop at `:245` happens to push in the same order; there is no structural guarantee, and `active` carries a batch index `b` it never uses for lookup. If any future change reorders/dedups/skips `active`, `.next()` returns the **wrong slot's** model-out; the `debug_assert_eq!` that would catch it is gone in release, so the engine **emits tenant A's codec frame on tenant B's egress** — the exact F3 cross-user privacy violation the whole lockstep design forbids — and the `stepped.remove().expect()` panics the *shared* mux loop (killing every concurrent tenant) when a continuing slot's id is missing.
- **Why it bites at enterprise scale.** A panic in the shared S2S loop is a whole-server outage (one loop serves all duplex streams), and the silent cross-wire is an undetectable-at-runtime data-confidentiality breach. Both are reachable from a single ordering refactor with no test gate in release.
- **Fix.** Address model-out by slot id, never position. Build `stepped` as the lookup of record (already a `HashMap<SlotId, StepOutput>`) and in the reassembly loop look up `slot.slot()` directly; drop the positional `active_iter` entirely. Replace both `expect`s with a typed `InferError::internal(...)` returned from `step` (the trait already returns `Result`), so a contract slip is a clean per-loop error, never a cross-tenant bleed or a shared-loop panic. Same outputs, addressed by id.

## HIGH

### [HIGH] The entire CUDA-graph-fallback / crash-isolation / poison-pill-firewall / long-form-KV / sampler resilience layer is unintegrated — the GB10 guarantees are inert `[confirmed]`
`accel.rs`, `cuda_graph.rs`, `kernel_discipline.rs`, `graph_fallback.rs`, `cell.rs` (whole files) · `paged_kv.rs`, `prefix_cache.rs` (whole files) · `numerics::sample_token` (`numerics.rs:280`) · `reasoning.rs` (whole file) · `watchdog.rs` legs `InputFirewall`@1411, `DeadLetterSink`@1657, `SourceRateLimiter`@1764, `RecycleGate`@1980, `CoMetric`@3254, `FrameBudget`@3422. All re-exported at `lib.rs:31-67`, all callerless outside `#[cfg(test)]`.
- **What's wrong.** Workspace grep (non-test, non-re-export) finds **zero** constructors for any of these. The H4 CUDA-graph fallback (doc: "NON-OPTIONAL on sm120", and GB10 capture *will* fail — multinomial sampler, varlen #45425, OOM #44209, hang #40969) is never instantiated. The J1 shared-context crash-isolation ("THE single highest-leverage architectural decision") never gates routing. The J17 poison-pill firewall + dead-letter + source-rate-limiter that stop one corrupt Opus frame from crash-looping a worker and taking down MPS neighbours are built and never wired. The L12 long-form paged-KV escape (module doc claims it "closes the hole") is dead; the live decode KV is the scheduler's `RingKvCache` fixed sliding window with **no pinned attention sink** (StreamingLLM collapse on multi-minute turns). The on-token `sample_token` is never called (live backends do in-graph/argmax selection).
- **Why it bites at enterprise scale.** Thousands of ragged streams on GB10 is exactly the regime these scars fire in. Shipping the *types* without the *wiring* is false assurance: standing-gate tests are green but prove host *policy*, not that the engine *uses* it. A real capture failure / corrupt frame / long session degrades or crashes with the documented fix sitting in dead code.
- **Fix.** Wire each at its declared seam (firewall at codec/STT ingress before GPU dispatch; `GraphFallback::record_capture_failure` into the ort-CUDA capture path; `CellTopology::route` into worker placement; `PagedKvTable` as the long-form escape behind `RingKvCache`), or down-rank the module docs from "production/closed" to "staged/unwired" and `#[doc(hidden)]`/`#[allow(dead_code)]` them with a tracking issue so the gap is explicit. Do **not** keep claiming "L12 closes the hole" / "H4 is non-optional" while the type is test-only.

### [HIGH] A vanished consumer keeps consuming GPU `step_batch` compute for the whole utterance — DoS amplification `[confirmed]`
`serve.rs:739-748` (active-rows filter is `!s.accum.done` only — never `hung_up`) · `serve.rs:519,839,845` (`hung_up` is set only inside `drain_finished_stream`, which runs at eos).
- **What's wrong.** In the multiplexed loop a stream's `hung_up` flag is set only when its sink returns `false` *during decode* — which happens only after the slot reaches eos. There is no path that marks a hung-up stream `done` mid-flight. So a stream whose consumer vanished at frame 5 keeps being gathered into `active_rows` and advanced through the batched `step_batch` for **every remaining stride up to `max_strides`**, producing codec frames + (eventually) decoding audio that nobody will ever receive. Cancellation *would* stop it (`FrameLoop` checks the token first), but a plain TCP disconnect does not auto-set the cancel token from inside the loop — the loop has no liveness probe; it relies entirely on the external WS canceller (`ws.rs:347` does call `cancel.cancel()` on hangup, which mitigates the *clean* WS-disconnect case, but a half-open socket / dropped forwarder / non-WS caller leaves the slot running).
- **Why it bites at enterprise scale.** Under a disconnect storm (mobile clients, load-balancer resets), every abandoned stream burns a full slot's worth of GPU for its entire `max_strides` budget. With `MAX_SLOTS = 24` and `max_strides` in the hundreds-to-thousands, a wave of disconnects pins the cohort on dead work, collapsing effective concurrency for live tenants — a cheap amplification (one dropped socket → thousands of wasted strides).
- **Fix.** Make egress hangup short-circuit the AR loop: when `hung_up` is set (or when a cheap per-tick liveness check on the egress channel reports `Closed`), mark `s.accum.done = true` with a `Cancelled`/`Hungup` exit so the slot leaves the active set next tick and frees for a waiting admission. The decode + audio synthesis for a hung-up stream should be skipped entirely (it already is, post-eos — but the *steps* before eos are the waste). Independent of bit-identity (a hung-up stream is discarded).

### [HIGH] Multiplexed + buffered scatter is O(active²) per tick — a linear `find` per output row `[confirmed]`
`serve.rs:755` (`live.iter_mut().filter_map(...).find(|s| s.slot == slot)` inside the per-output loop) and `serve.rs:1021` (`accums.iter_mut().enumerate().find(|(_, a)| a.slot == slot)`).
- **What's wrong.** After `driver.tick` returns one `StepOutput` per active slot, the loop scatters each output back into its `LiveStream`/`SlotAccum` by **linearly scanning the slot vector for a matching slot id** — for every output. That is O(active) per output × active outputs = **O(active²) per tick**. At `MAX_SLOTS = 24` it is a 24×24 scan per frame; if the cohort cap is ever raised (the whole point of the batcher is to scale the cohort) it scales quadratically against the lockstep tick rate.
- **Why it bites.** It is pure per-tick overhead on the hottest path, competing with the model `step_batch` for the same core, and it scales worse than the O(active) the lockstep design promises. It also re-borrows `live` mutably inside the `outs` loop, forcing the find each iteration.
- **Fix (bit-faithful).** `driver.tick` already returns outputs in the active-row order it was given, and the active rows were built from `live`/`accums` in index order — so scatter by *index*, not by id search. Carry the slot's vector index alongside the `SlotInput` (or zip the outputs with the same filtered iterator that built `active_rows`), turning the scatter into O(active) direct indexing. Same outputs.

### [HIGH] Datagram `MediaSeq` wraparound: `cursor + depth` overflows and `seq < cursor` treats a wrapped/reset sender as "all stale" → permanent silence or OOM `[confirmed]` `[shelfware: DatagramJitterBuffer has no live caller]`
`datagram.rs:288` (`if seq > cursor + self.depth as u64` — overflows u64 near the top of the range; a release wrap makes the test false → a far-future datagram is buffered *outside* the bound, re-opening the OOM the module exists to close) · `datagram.rs:274` (`if seq < cursor` → every post-wrap/post-reset datagram is `TooLate`, stream goes permanently silent with no terminal) · `datagram.rs:109` (`next() = self.0 + 1` overflow-panics at `u64::MAX`).
- **What's wrong.** The buffer orders purely by raw `u64` magnitude with no serial-number arithmetic and no overflow guard. `MediaSeq` is sender-stamped and on the wire (attacker-controlled). A crafted seq near `u64::MAX`, or a peer that restarts its sequence (SSRC change / reconnect mapped onto the same `ChannelId`), either panics the receive path (debug), silently defeats the bound (release wrap), or drops every subsequent frame forever.
- **Why it bites.** The module's own docstring promises "a test that drives it can never hang … never a panic" — this is the counterexample, on adversarial wire input, which is the only place an unguarded `+`/`-` meets hostile data.
- **Fix.** Use checked/saturating math everywhere seq meets cursor: `if seq.saturating_sub(cursor) > self.depth as u64`, `new_cursor = seq.saturating_sub(self.depth as u64)`, `next() = self.0.wrapping_add(1)`. For the reset/wrap case, either document + enforce "seq is per-session monotonic; an epoch change MUST `reset()`" (the `reset()` at `:380` exists) or adopt RFC-3550 serial arithmetic. (Shelfware today, but it is the runtime's designated jitter buffer — fix before wiring.)

### [HIGH] `precision.rs` resolver is graph-driven but its one live consumer silently narrows every non-F16 KV dtype to F32 — crashes the next bf16/q4f16 model on CUDA `[confirmed]`
`precision.rs:45-54` (`empty_kv_dtype` returns the graph's declared `ElemType`, correctly) · consumer `waav-infer-core/src/stt/voxtral.rs:31-34` (`if empty_kv_dtype_for(g, name) == ElemType::F16 { f16 } else { f32 }`).
- **What's wrong.** `ElemType` has six variants (`F32, F16, I64, I32, Bool, Other`; no `BF16` — a bf16 export maps to `Other`). The resolver faithfully reports the declared type, but the sole live consumer collapses everything that is not exactly `F16` to `F32`. A graph that declares an empty KV/position/padding-state input as **bf16 (`Other`)** or **i64/i32** gets a silently-wrong **F32** empty tensor fed into the node — the exact dtype-mismatch crash class `precision.rs` exists to prevent, just shifted from "f32-into-q4f16" to "f32-into-anything-not-f16".
- **Why it bites.** The project is actively onboarding q4f16/bf16 variants (see MEMORY). The moment one lands, the "zero-code weight swap" promise crashes on CUDA at first decode for every stream on that model.
- **Fix.** Make `zeros_typed` exhaustive over the resolved `ElemType` (build f16/bf16/i64/i32/f32 zeros via `match`), or have `empty_kv_dtype` return a typed error for a dtype the empty-tensor builder cannot honor. Honor the graph dtype on every path, not just the F16 fast-arm. (Also tighten `precision.rs:26-31`'s `input_name.contains(f)` feature match to exact/suffix — see MED — so a KV input that merely embeds a feature substring isn't force-narrowed.)

### [HIGH] `ComputeLease` leaks duty on `Drop` — a leaked lease permanently starves the reasoning pool `[confirmed]` `[cross-crate, but the runtime's reasoning nodes are the intended holders]`
`waav-infer-scheduler/src/lease.rs:63` (struct, no `Drop` impl) · `:212` (`return_lease`). Also `02-scheduler.md`.
- **What's wrong.** `ComputeLease` reserves duty in `ComputeLedger` and is freed **only** by an explicit `return_lease`/`cancel`. There is no `Drop` impl; `#[must_use]` only warns on an ignored return value, not on a lease moved into a struct (e.g. the reasoning `LlmStreamNode.lease: Option<ComputeLease>`) that is then dropped on an error/panic/early-`?` path. The doc even admits "dropping it without returning leaks the reservation (the duty stays charged forever)."
- **Why it bites.** The reasoning-LLM admission pool is bounded (`Σduty ≤ bound`). Every dropped-not-returned lease permanently subtracts from the free pool; `return_lease` saturates at 0 so an over-count is sticky. Under a barge-in storm / panicking off-clock pump, `Σduty` ratchets up and never recovers → `try_admit` returns `Saturated` for *every* session box-wide → all reasoning permanently refused until process restart.
- **Fix.** Give `ComputeLease` a `Drop` that returns its duty as a fallback (hold an `Arc<Mutex<LedgerInner>>`/`Weak` back-ref; guard with a `returned: bool` set in `return_lease` to avoid double-subtract). Standard RAII guard; no numeric change. (This bites whenever the reasoning nodes are wired — the runtime `reasoning.rs` nodes are the intended holders, currently shelfware.)

### [HIGH] `SentenceAggregator` buffer is unbounded + ASCII-only terminal punctuation — OOM on a punctuation-free stream and never partial-fires for CJK/Indic `[confirmed]` `[shelfware]`
`reasoning.rs:218-240` (`push` does `self.buf.push_str(tok)`, clears only on `ends_clause`) · `:253-256` (`ends_clause` matches only `'.' | '?' | '!'`). Live twin: scheduler `LlmStreamNode.agg` (`lease.rs:303`).
- **What's wrong.** A reasoning LLM that streams without an ASCII terminal — a runaway generation, a code block, or *any* CJK/Indic output (`。？！…।` never match) — grows `buf` without bound and never emits a clause until end-of-turn.
- **Why it bites.** Per-stream unbounded `String` growth across thousands of sessions is a direct OOM vector on the shared 121 GB pool (MEMORY flags GB10 unified-memory OOM as a box-killer). Independently, it is a correctness/latency bug: Chinese/Japanese/Hindi reasoning never partial-fires, defeating the latency win the aggregator exists for.
- **Fix.** Add CJK/Indic terminal puncts to `ends_clause` (unicode-aware), and add a max-buffer-chars cap that force-flushes a clause (or rejects typed) when exceeded. Cap-flush is bit-faithful for normal input.

### [HIGH] No tool-call deadline — a hung tool pins a session in `Running` forever `[confirmed]` `[shelfware]`
`reasoning.rs:450-573` (entire `ToolCallNode`: `Running → Ready → Merged/Dropped`, no timeout).
- **What's wrong.** `complete` is driven only by an external executor; if that executor hangs (slow HTTP tool, wedged subprocess, vendor stall), the node stays `Running` indefinitely and `resume` perpetually returns `None` — the turn can never commit. The cancel token is cooperative (only stops an executor that checks it; a wedged one never does). There is no `now`-injected deadline like `EagerStage`/`LatencyFiller` have.
- **Why it bites.** "Tool hang" is an explicit missing scenario; at scale one hung upstream tool leaks a session forever with no automatic reaping.
- **Fix.** Add `started_at` + `deadline_ms` and a `poll(now)` that transitions `Running → TimedOut`/`Dropped` (setting the cancel token) past the deadline — mirror `EagerStage::fire_if_confident`'s `now`-injected discipline. Purely additive control logic. (Same zombie-watchdog gap also affects `LatencyFiller` getting stuck `Firing` forever, `reasoning.rs:173-201` — see MED.)

### [HIGH] `RecycleGate.states` and the leaked-channel ledger have NO eviction path — watchdog-side memory leaks under stream churn `[confirmed]` `[RecycleGate is shelfware; LeakWatchdog reconcile is live]`
`watchdog.rs:1961` (`RecycleGate.states: BTreeMap<ChannelId, RecycleState>` — inserted at `:2024/:2028`, never `remove`/`retain`/`clear_channel`) · `watchdog.rs:356` (`LeakWatchdog::reconcile` is non-destructive — a leaked channel's ledger entry is never reclaimed; entry removed only by explicit `ChannelLease::free` at `:454`, `Drop` at `:466` deliberately no-ops).
- **What's wrong.** `ChannelId` is minted fresh per stream (`u64`). `RecycleGate` is insert-and-mutate-only, so every channel that ever requests a recycle leaves a permanent entry (unlike its siblings `FrameWatchdog`/`InputFirewall`/`SourceRateLimiter`/`SidecarHeartbeat`, which all have a `clear_channel`/`shed`/`deregister`). Separately, `LeakWatchdog::reconcile` (run every 1 s by the production driver at `lib.rs` and only reading `leaked_count()`) re-counts and retains every *actually-leaked* channel forever — the leak gauge climbs monotonically and the map grows unbounded, compounding the original slot leak with a watchdog-side leak.
- **Why it bites.** Slow per-stream memory leaks invisible until OOM hours/days in — the churn-of-short-streams scenario. `RecycleGate` is a landmine the moment it is wired; the `LeakWatchdog` reconcile leak is live today (latent only because real leaks are presumed rare).
- **Fix.** Add `RecycleGate::clear_channel(channel)` (mirror the siblings) called on stream end. Give the 1 s reconcile loop a `reconcile_and_drain()` (or a TTL/age field) that reclaims reported entries after they are alarmed, so a sustained leak cannot grow the ledger without limit. Keep the non-destructive `reconcile` for the "poll, watch it shrink" use.

### [HIGH] `IdleSignal::notify` wakes only ONE parked waiter and latches a single bool — a shared signal across multiple loops can lose wakeups `[suspected]`
`driver.rs:122-129` (`notify` → `cvar.notify_one()`, flag is a single `bool`).
- **What's wrong.** `notify()` calls `notify_one()` and sets one shared `bool`. `Driver::with_idle_signal` (`driver.rs:211`) explicitly supports an externally-owned signal shared by an admission path and the loop. If more than one consumer ever parks on the same `IdleSignal` (e.g. a future multi-loop batcher, or the loop racing a control thread that also waits), a single `notify` wakes exactly one and the latch is consumed once — the other stays parked until its bounded short-sleep (5 ms) elapses. With N admissions arriving as one burst, only one wake is guaranteed; the rest rely on the 5 ms re-check.
- **Why it bites.** Today the live design is one loop per signal (`codec_ar_batcher.rs:322`), so this is latent — but the bounded 5 ms re-check masks it, meaning a correctness regression here would surface only as a latency hiccup, not a hang, making it hard to catch. The API shape (shared signal) invites the bug.
- **Fix.** If a shared signal is ever multi-waiter, use `notify_all()` and a per-waiter generation counter (or a small counter instead of a `bool` latch) so every parked consumer observes the edge. Document the "single waiter per signal" precondition explicitly until then.

## MED

### [MED] Egress single-stream path uses a tokio **unbounded** channel — a slow WS consumer grows memory without bound `[confirmed]`
`waav-infer-server/src/engine.rs:1066` (`tokio::sync::mpsc::unbounded_channel::<StreamItem>()`), sink at `serve_codec_ar_stream` does `tx.send(item).is_ok()`.
- **What's wrong.** The single-stream serve avoids the CRITICAL-1 block by using an *unbounded* tokio channel — so it never blocks the producer, but a slow/stalled WS reader lets the channel grow without bound (the whole utterance buffers in RAM, per stream). It is the inverse trade of the mux path: no head-of-line stall, but no memory bound either.
- **Why it bites.** Thousands of single-stream TTS requests with slow readers each buffer a full utterance of PCM16 — unbounded resident memory, the OOM the mux path's bounded channel was careful to avoid.
- **Fix.** Use a bounded tokio channel for the single-stream egress too, with a non-blocking `try_send`-then-shed policy (or the off-loop forwarder from CRITICAL-1's fix). Pick *one* backpressure story across both paths.

### [MED] `StreamEgress.witness` retains every codec id / PCM sample for the whole utterance — two full body copies resident per long stream `[confirmed]`
`egress.rs:166` (`witness: Witness`), folded every `push` at `:238/:253`; `serve.rs:110` (`SlotAccum.body`) accumulates the same body in parallel.
- **What's wrong.** The cumulative witness keeps the entire stream's product until `finish`/`reset`, *and* the serve loop's `SlotAccum.body` holds the same body — so a long utterance holds two full copies at native precision. There is no `max_strides`-equivalent bound inside egress; it relies entirely on the caller's stride cap. The witness exists only for the I1 offline-equality *test* gate.
- **Why it bites.** Thousands of concurrent long streams × two full-body copies × native precision = the memory blow-up the bounded datagram window avoided, reintroduced on the egress side.
- **Fix.** Gate the witness behind `#[cfg(test)]` / an opt-in constructor so the live path ships deltas without retaining the cumulative copy. Deltas unchanged; only the internal witness drops. Also `pub(crate)`/test-gate `full_codes`/`full_pcm`/`take_terminal` (`egress.rs:303-320`) so callers can't depend on the cumulative buffer.

### [MED] `AcousticDelayRing` casts `offset: u64` to `i32` for ring indexing — wraps negative on a multi-hour stream `[suspected]` `[shelfware]`
`arstep.rs:387` (`(self.offset as i32 + self.delays[k]).rem_euclid(...)`) and `arstep.rs:396` (`(self.offset as i32 - self.max_delay + self.gen_delays[k]).rem_euclid(...)`).
- **What's wrong.** `offset` is a monotonic `u64` frame counter; `widx`/`ridx` cast it to `i32` before the modulo. Past `i32::MAX` frames (~2.1 B; at 12.5–75 Hz that is days-to-weeks of continuous generation) the cast wraps negative. `rem_euclid` still yields an in-range index, but potentially the **wrong cell** — a silent correctness drift on a very-long-lived ring, not a panic.
- **Why it bites.** Only for pathologically long single occupancies, and the ring is shelfware today — but the cast is gratuitous: the modulo could be done in `u64`/`usize` arithmetic directly.
- **Fix.** Compute the ring position in `u64` (`(self.offset + delay).rem_euclid(ct)` with `delay` as `u64`, handling the read-side subtraction with a `+ct` bias before the modulo) so the index is exact for any `offset`. Bit-identical for all in-range offsets.

### [MED] Datagram forced-advance / `drain_through` materialize one loss-marker per skipped seq — O(gap) work + allocation from a single ingest `[confirmed]` `[shelfware]`
`datagram.rs:357-362` (`for seq in from..new_cursor { push Lost }`) and `:329-341` (`while self.cursor < up_to { push Lost }`).
- **What's wrong.** A single `ForcedAdvance`/`drain_through` whose target jumped by a large delta loops once per missing seq, pushing a `PlayoutFrame::Lost` each. A stream whose first datagram arrives at a high seq (resumed session, or hostile) triggers a multi-million-iteration loop + multi-million-element `Vec` synchronously in ingest. The docstring claim "a forced advance adds at most `depth` markers" is false when the cursor was far behind.
- **Fix.** Cap the loss-marker run at `depth` (or a fixed concealment horizon) and jump the cursor directly. Only ≤depth frames can have been buffered; the unbounded part is purely the missing-seq marker generation.

### [MED] Sampler top-p renormalization silently skipped when the nucleus sums to 0 → returns the worst-of-nucleus token, not the distribution `[confirmed]` `[shelfware]`
`numerics.rs:349-356` (`if s > 0.0 { renormalize }`; the `s == 0.0` path falls through to the multinomial draw over an un-normalized vector and returns `probs.last()` at `:368`).
- **What's wrong.** If extreme temperature pushes the truncated nucleus to all-subnormal that `truncate` drops, `s == 0.0` and the distribution is left un-normalized; the draw's `cum` never reaches `r`, so it deterministically returns the last (lowest-prob) kept token — the opposite of what top-p asked. No panic (the `unwrap_or(cand[0].0)` saves it), but silently wrong, and in an AR loop the wrong token compounds (#2274).
- **Fix.** Handle `s <= 0.0` explicitly: return `cand[0].0` (argmax fallback, matching the `sum <= 0.0` branch at `:329-333`) *before* the draw. (Also: `sample_token` does a full O(vocab) `check_finite_slice` + 2× O(vocab) `Vec` + full `sort` per token — fuse the finite-check into the argmax pass and use `select_nth_unstable_by` for top-k; see LOW. All shelfware until the AR loop is wired to it.)

### [MED] CUDA-graph re-capture commits `self.captured` BEFORE the backend confirms capture — next tick replays a graph that was never recorded `[confirmed]` `[shelfware]`
`cuda_graph.rs:286-292` (`CudaGraphTier::plan` sets `self.captured = Some(key)` and returns `CaptureEdge(key)` in one step; no "capture confirmed" callback).
- **What's wrong.** On a shape change, `plan` optimistically records `captured = Some(key)`. If the backend's capture then fails (the H4 scar), the policy already believes it holds a valid graph; the next matching tick returns `ReplayEdge(key)` → the backend replays a graph that was never successfully captured (stale/never-recorded buffer reuse — silent corruption / illegal access). `GraphFallback` would mask this *if wired*, but `GraphFallback` is itself dead, and `CudaGraphTier` is independently `pub`/usable.
- **Fix.** `plan` returns `CaptureEdge` without mutating `captured`; add `confirm_captured(key)` the backend calls only on success. Until confirmed, re-plan `CaptureEdge`, never `ReplayEdge` — making "replay an uncaptured graph" structurally impossible. (Also: `EdgeCaptureKey` keys only on `(b, stride_width)` — `cuda_graph.rs:148-203` — not dtype/device, so two mixed-precision cohorts of the same shape would cross-replay; fix by including dtype in the key.)

### [MED] `kernel_discipline` exactness mechanism (`mask_pad_rows`) covers only the softmax case — a padded GEMM contraction dim relies on unverified backend zero-fill `[confirmed]` `[shelfware]`
`kernel_discipline.rs:296-307` (`mask_pad_rows` builds a `-inf` softmax mask) · doc `:298-300` concedes "a pure-GEMM with no softmax recovers exactness from the zero-pad alone".
- **What's wrong.** The module's masked≠absent bit-identity rule-2 claims "zero-pad + −inf mask ⇒ bit-for-bit", but the only primitive it ships (`mask_pad_rows`) is meaningful only when the padded dim feeds a softmax. For a padded **K (contraction)** dim of a plain GEMM (QKV/MLP projections — most padded GEMMs in the AR step), exactness depends entirely on the pad lanes being genuinely **zero** in both operands — and this module provides no zeroing primitive and no check. If the backend pads K with uninitialized memory, `0 * NaN = NaN` poisons the real reduction — the exact masked≠absent kernel bug, in the blind spot.
- **Fix.** Add an explicit `zero_pad_lanes(buf, pad)` + a contract test that the pad region is bit-zero before the GEMM, OR narrow the doc claim to "attention-score padding only" and document K-dim pad-zeroing as the backend's tested contract. Don't assert "bit-for-bit for any GEMM" while shipping only the softmax half. (Also `FusionPlan` cannot represent bf16 — `ElemType` has no `BF16`, so an fp32-weight-mul-on-bf16 model `(Other, Other)` is silently accepted, defeating the #42325 inverse-trap guard for the model class that needs it most.)

### [MED] Cell crash-quarantine has no clock and no budget reset — a long-lived cell quarantined permanently on its 2nd transient fault `[confirmed]` `[shelfware]`
`cell.rs:150` (`MAX_RESTARTS_BEFORE_QUARANTINE = 1`) · `:256-264` (verdict `identical_recrash && restarts >= 1`) · `:294` (`restarts` monotonic, never decremented/aged).
- **What's wrong.** `restarts` never decrements or ages out, and the quarantine rule has no time component (module is "sync, no clock"). A cell that crashed on `IllegalAddress`, restarted, served fine for 6 hours, then hit a *new unrelated* `IllegalAddress` (ECC blip, thermal) is quarantined permanently ("only operator action brings it back"). The poison-pill signature is *tight temporal recurrence*, which the logic cannot distinguish from two faults hours apart.
- **Fix.** Gate quarantine on crash *rate*: carry a last-crash timestamp, treat an identical re-crash as a loop only within a short window, and reset `restarts` after a clean-serve cooldown. (Also: `route(&self)` vs `crash(&mut self)` is a TOCTOU once the single-writer topology is shared across route/fault threads — `cell.rs:214-247`; decide and document the concurrency model, e.g. `AtomicU8` health with Acquire/Release, before wiring.)

### [MED] `SidecarHeartbeat::check_at` MUTATES (latches Dead) despite a doc/name identical to the read-only `FrameWatchdog::check_at` — a far-future `now` mass-kills every sidecar `[confirmed]` `[live]`
`watchdog.rs:2835` (`iter_mut`, sets `rec.health = Dead` at `:2846`) vs `FrameWatchdog::check_at`@647 (read-only). Tests at `torch_sidecar.rs:576/650/667` call `check_at(Duration::from_secs(3600))` to *force* a death verdict.
- **What's wrong.** Two `check_at` methods with the same "out-of-band pass" prose have opposite mutation semantics. A caller reasoning "`check_at` is non-destructive, poll freely" (true for the dominant `FrameWatchdog`) will, on `SidecarHeartbeat`, irreversibly latch sidecars Dead. A far-future `now` (clock skew, VM-migration monotonic jump, or a test-style large value leaking to prod) declares *every* `Live` sidecar dead in one pass — a correlated fleet-wide failover storm.
- **Fix.** Rename to `check_and_latch_at` (or split a pure `would_be_dead_at` predicate from the latching action), align the doc-comment to say it mutates, and document the far-future-`now` mass-trip hazard.

### [MED] Stall/heartbeat deadline `frame_interval.saturating_mul(grace_ticks)` can saturate to `Duration::MAX` → the silent-hang watchdog never trips `[confirmed]` `[live]`
`watchdog.rs:548` (`StallDeadline::window`) and `:2685` (`HeartbeatDeadline::window`).
- **What's wrong.** The doc says saturating avoids an *absurdly tiny* window, but the opposite failure it creates is worse: a large `frame_interval × grace_ticks` saturates to `Duration::MAX`, so `silent_for > window` is never true and the stall/dead-sidecar watchdog **silently never fires**. A config typo (seconds-for-millis × a big grace) disables "the ONLY defense against silent hangs" (the file's own words, J16) with no error — fails open.
- **Fix.** Validate at construction that the product does not saturate (reject as `BadConfig` if `>= Duration::MAX`, or cap `grace_ticks`), so an over-large deadline is a typed config error, not a dead watchdog.

### [MED] `LatencyFiller` can stick `Firing` forever — masks a dead reasoning stream with no timeout `[confirmed]` `[shelfware]`
`reasoning.rs:173-201` (`Firing → Done` only via `on_first_reasoning_sentence`; `Firing → Idle` only via `cancel`).
- **What's wrong.** If the reasoning tier dies *after* the filler fires but *before* its first sentence (LLM crash, leaked lease, dropped connection) and the user does not barge in, the filler stays `Firing` with no path out and no timeout. The holding clip ends, the user hears silence forever while the FSM claims it is masking.
- **Fix.** Add a `now`-injected max-firing-duration that transitions `Firing → Done`/`Idle` if no reasoning sentence arrives within a bound (the zombie-watchdog discipline MEMORY names the #1 SDK gap). Additive.

### [MED] `TurnState` has no client-gone transition — a vanished client mid-`Yielding`/`Speaking` strands the floor (depends on the un-reviewed driver to reap) `[suspected]` `[live]`
`turn.rs:163-171` (`DoubleTalkPolicy::decide` is a pure per-tick function of `(user_vad, model_speaking)`; no disconnect notion). Consumed live at `duplex.rs:806`, `duplex_codec_ar.rs:318`.
- **What's wrong.** `decide` has no "client disconnected → release floor" transition and no timeout. If the upstream driver stops calling `decide` (client vanished, `user_vad` frozen), the last-computed `TurnState` sticks. The live duplex path depends entirely on the (un-reviewed) slot lifecycle to reap a vanished client; if that relies solely on `decide`, a frozen `user_vad` strands the turn holding the floor.
- **Fix.** Ensure the slot lifecycle has a disconnect deadline; optionally add a `Released`/`Closed` terminal `TurnState` so "floor relinquished due to client-gone" is representable rather than inferred from a frozen `Speaking`. Verify the duplex driver's client-liveness watchdog.

### [MED] `EagerStage::rollback` returns a stale `committed_offset` on a double-rollback → a driver that truncates KV "to the returned offset" re-truncates valid history `[suspected]` `[live-adjacent]`
`turn.rs:204-207` (`rollback` clears `fired` and unconditionally returns `self.committed_offset`).
- **What's wrong.** A stage that fired at offset 100, rolled back (returns 100, `fired=false`), then rolled back *again* without a re-fire returns the **stale 100**, not a "nothing staged" sentinel. A driver truncating KV to the returned offset on every rollback would truncate to 100 even though nothing is staged → silent KV corruption. The doc claims idempotency but the returned value isn't idempotent-safe.
- **Fix.** Return `Option<u64>` (`None` when not fired) or reset `committed_offset = 0` on rollback so a double-rollback returns the harmless 0. (Live consumer not yet found — currently shelfware in a live module.)

### [MED] `DcgmExporterDramActive` integrator `cumulative_bytes: f64` grows unboundedly → bus-cost calibration silently dies on a multi-week box `[confirmed]` `[live]`
`watchdog.rs:2487` (`cumulative_bytes: f64`), advanced at `:2530`, clamped to `u64::MAX` at `:2537`.
- **What's wrong.** The accumulator only grows; over weeks it climbs toward `u64::MAX as f64`, after which every window delta the `CoLoadCalibrator` computes is 0 (two saturated reads), silently disabling bus-cost calibration (falls back to the conservative roofline — admission-safe but the calibration is dead). `f64` also loses integer precision above 2^53 bytes (~9 PB) long before saturation.
- **Fix.** Store the delta against the window-open scrape rather than an unbounded running total, or rebase periodically, or document the multi-week saturation horizon as acceptable.

### [MED] `MuxAdmit.utterance` is cloned on every prefill; `decode_audio` body held twice; per-tick `active_rows` Vec allocation `[confirmed]`
`serve.rs:683` (`&stream.admit.utterance.clone()`) · `serve.rs:739/995` (`active_rows: Vec<SlotInput>` allocated every tick) · plus the live `CodecArDuplexModel::step` allocates 3 collections per tick (`duplex_codec_ar.rs:244/264/281`).
- **What's wrong.** Steady per-tick / per-admission heap allocation on the hot path: the utterance `String` is cloned redundantly at prefill (it is owned by `admit`, borrowed immutably — the clone is gratuitous), the active-rows `SlotInput` Vec is freshly allocated every tick (bounded by cohort but every frame), and the live duplex `step` allocates an `active` Vec + `continuing` Vec + `stepped` HashMap per tick.
- **Why it bites.** Allocator pressure across thousands of ticks/sec competing with `step_batch` for bandwidth on the shared unified-memory pool.
- **Fix.** Borrow the utterance into `prefill` (drop the clone); reuse a fixed-capacity scratch `Vec<SlotInput>` across ticks (the slot vector is fixed `[MAX_SLOTS]`); in the duplex `step`, reuse scratch buffers and (after CRITICAL-2's id-keyed fix) index by position instead of a `HashMap`.

## What is genuinely solid (so the fixes don't regress it — and so the next reviewer doesn't re-chase these)

- **`driver.rs` is clean.** `ActiveSet::new` rejects a double-stepped slot with a typed error (no panic), `tick` parks on a real blocking `Condvar` (no busy-spin), the lost-wakeup latch is `Mutex`-guarded and tested, `step_batch` length-mismatch is a typed error, masked≠absent is honored (the active set excludes finished/masked rows by construction). The only nit is the shared-signal `notify_one` (HIGH-suspected).
- **`arstep.rs` seam is impeccable.** `CodecFrame`/`StepOutput` out-of-range accessors return `None` (never index-panic), `try_new` rejects empty/ragged typed, `step_batch` default is the bit-identical per-slot fallback, `decode_audio_stream` enforces the whole-body bit-identity law, `AcousticDelayRing` validates config + leaves the ring byte-identical on a rejected write + `reset` is recycle-clean. (Only the i32-offset cast, MED, and it's shelfware.)
- **`serve.rs` spine RAII is correct.** `SpineSlots::Drop` frees every lease + sheds every watchdog entry on any `?` early-return (the zombie-slot backstop), the clean path settles explicitly, the `max_strides` truncation boundary is `strides > max_strides` (the off-by-one a ragged live gate caught — documented at `:716-726`), per-slot cancel/truncate does not desync in-bound neighbours, and the bounded `pending` queue sheds overflow with a typed retriable terminal (`shed_admission`). Bit-identity to the per-slot reference is live-gated (`multiplexed_streams_admitted_at_different_times_share_the_loop`).
- **`watchdog.rs` arithmetic is over-defended.** Zero raw `-` on the live path (every site `checked_sub`/`saturating_*`); all 8 `lock()` helpers recover from poison via `unwrap_or_else(|p| p.into_inner())` (the "one panic poisons the shared map" hypothesis is **refuted** — there is no `.lock().unwrap()` on a `PoisonError` on the live path); no `.await` held across a lock (module is genuinely sync); no busy-spin; no re-entrant lock-across-callback (the ORT-init-style deadlock does not recur). Every overloaded component sheds with a typed `Err`, never hangs.
- **masked≠absent / step==per-slot bit-identity** is the central invariant and is live-gated token-for-token on real CUDA for ragged cohorts (`s2s_duplex_ragged_concurrent_batched_bit_identical_and_scales`, `live_gb10_batcher_concurrent_ragged_is_bit_identical_and_scales`), including the frozen-masked-neighbour no-crosstalk gate.
- **`CancelToken`** is a clean `Arc<AtomicBool>` (Acquire/Release), minted fresh per `MuxAdmit` — no cross-slot cancel-poisoning on slot reuse. **`FrameLoop::next_frame`** checks the token first, mints a non-`Copy` `FramePermit` (a cancelled frame is unrepresentable). **`SampleRng`** (SplitMix64) is deterministic + per-slot (reproducibility holds). **`cfg_batch.rs`** is pure typed-reject policy (no per-tick alloc). **`dynamic_fr.rs`** rejects `stride=0` typed and the loop's active set empties monotonically (no infinite loop). **`CfmCoeffCache`** is bit-faithful + rejects 0-step/OOB-step typed.

## Severity rationale & cross-references
- **CRITICAL-1** (shared-loop block) is the only finding that degrades the *running* server today under a benign trigger (a slow reader); it is the load-bearing answer to "slow/disconnecting consumers" and is also filed in `04-server-integration.md`.
- **CRITICAL-2** (cross-tenant bleed) is a confidentiality + availability defect on the live S2S path, reachable from an ordinary refactor with no release-mode test gate.
- **HIGH-1** (unwired resilience layer) mirrors `02-scheduler.md` CRITICAL-1 and `00-SYNTHESIS.md`: the dominant *architectural* risk is coverage, not micro-bugs — the engine runs the un-reviewed path while the reviewed path is dead. Every shelfware HIGH/MED is filed at its when-live severity precisely because the wiring is the stated M5 direction.
- The remaining live HIGH/MED (hung-consumer GPU waste, O(active²) scatter, datagram seq overflow, precision narrowing, watchdog-map leaks, deadline-saturation, `check_at` mass-kill) are the defects that bite the *live* spine under enterprise load and should be fixed regardless of the integration question.
